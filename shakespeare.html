    PLAYWRIGHT VANISHES ON EVE OF PREMIERE

    William Shakespeare left his Silver Street lodgings last night and did not return. This afternoon his company, the King’s Men, are rehearsing Macbeth at the Globe — but the final scene is missing.

    His landlady says his candle is still burning. The King’s censor says the play is dangerous and wants the ending changed. In the taverns, some claim a rival is behind it, but nobody has proof.

    A pedlar is selling a “lost scene”, yet the page is written in modern English on lined paper — a forgery this newsroom will not print. What is certain is small; what is rumour is large. The curtain rises at two o’clock.
    🕵 Debrief: what was planted?
    The pedlar’s ‘lost scene’. The words may sound like Shakespeare, but modern English and lined paper give it away. Real forgeries happened: William-Henry Ireland faked Shakespeare papers in the 1790s and fooled experts. True ≠ authentic.

    The ‘coffee-house’ (Stationers’ Hall). Coffee-houses only reached London around 1650 — over forty years too late for 1606 → anachronism.

    Why records matter. Macbeth and half of Shakespeare’s plays survive only because friends printed the First Folio in 1623. Without that source, the story would have been lost — which is exactly why a newsroom guards its sources.
