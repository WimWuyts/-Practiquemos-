    TITANIC SINKS ON HER MAIDEN VOYAGE

    The Titanic struck an iceberg at 23:40 on 14 April 1912 and sank less than three hours later. At 02:20 the ship went under; hundreds of people were in the water.

    Earlier, the White Star Line said there was no danger, but passengers reported that water was coming into the lower decks. The Carpathia picked up the distress call and turned around, but she was still four hours away.

    The Carpathia now says it rescued about 705 people. More than 1,500 are still missing. (A “radar” report and a “smartphone” photograph arrived too, but the newsroom did not publish them — neither source could be real in 1912.)
    🕵 Debrief: what was wrong?
    The radar (00:12). Radar was only used from around the Second World War. The word alone does not belong in 1912 → anachronism.

    The smartphone photo (01:20). The image may be right, but smartphones did not exist. True ≠ authentic: the source cannot possibly be real.

    “Everyone is safe” (08:30). This really happened: the first newspapers in April 1912 reported that everyone had been saved. A period-authentic source with an interest can simply lie → manipulation, not an anachronism.
